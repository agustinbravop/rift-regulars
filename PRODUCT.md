# Rift Regulars

## Register

product

## Users

League of Legends players looking up their Riot ID to recognize recurring teammates and opponents.

## Product Purpose

Answer “Who have I met most often?” using retrievable matches from the current calendar year. Landing and header search ask only for Name#TAG and discover the active League platform through Account-V1, starting on Americas without tag/IP inference. Riot requests run on the server; compact match history stays in the browser's IndexedDB, separated by account PUUID and year. The detected server is routing/display metadata. Refresh lists the current platform's regional host and downloads missing games, retaining cached matches from other platforms. Count two or more encounters across all queues and cached platforms, then show each player's shared matches in one chronological table. No regional-host fanout or complete transfer-history promise: live cross-region discovery and transfer PUUID continuity remain unverified, and different PUUIDs are not merged. A current-game lookup is a later feature.

Featured streamer accounts are an exception to browser-only history: a sequential operator command saves compact gzip histories privately on the server and publishes completed precomputed public snapshots. The landing catalog is static: all nine accounts appear once in two centered wrapping lists of five and four, with content-sized cards and generous gaps. Available cards open shared profiles at the same Name#TAG URLs without Riot calls or browser imports. Unimported cards have no link, action, count or unavailable message; direct links with missing data still show honest unavailability. Visitors cannot refresh shared profiles. Personal lookup remains unchanged. All participants, including singletons, remain in private checkpoints for future recurrence counting. New imports exclude custom queue 0, empty and out-of-year records; coverage states that retrievable history may not span the full year.

## Brand Personality

Public name: Rift Regulars. Headline: “Know the names you keep seeing on Summoner’s Rift.”

Practical, readable, compact. A match tracker, not a marketing site.

A full-width sortable player list with inline expandable row details is the chosen layout. Default to dark, with OP.GG and coachless.gg as visual references, while retaining larger text and controls.

## Anti-references

No eyebrows, extra marketing sections beyond the search hero and functional featured-player carousel, decorative effects, or separate dossier/sidebar. No unsupported premade claims. No dense tiny-text tables. No reset-filter button.

## Design Principles

- Rank by actual encounters, not speculative scores.
- Show coverage and incomplete imports honestly.
- Keep names from match data without extra Riot ID lookups.
- Make queue and teammate/opponent differences easy to inspect.
- Premade labels use manual marks or the user-requested rule of at least five games together and strictly more than 95% together across all imported encounters, including remakes. Exactly 95% does not qualify. This is a heuristic, not Riot party detection. Manual unmarks override the rule.
- Treat Bravery Arena (1740) and regular Arena (1750) as one Arena queue for display, filters and detail counts, including the two-encounter threshold. Keep original queue IDs and metadata in saved data.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Larger text and spacing, keyboard support, readable contrast, and explicit text for wins and losses.
